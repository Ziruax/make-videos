# c3d-1 — "The Shell"

`/make-cutaway-3d` proof piece. 1080×1920 @ **30fps** (the 3D cadence), 780 frames
= 26.0s, Three.js cutaway of the Earth's interior — the dive-to-the-core film.

## The story

The Earth as a cutaway object: a calm matte globe whose wedge opens on the first
act turn; the camera dives through mantle and liquid-iron outer core (the churning
that generates the magnetic field, visualized as ivory field lines blooming);
settles at the gold inner core — a ball of SOLID iron as hot as the sun's surface;
the layers explode into a labelled vertical stack, reassemble mirrored, and the
wedge closes on the opening framing. The end is the start, changed: you now know
what the shell contains.

**The device (sayable):** the planet opens like a fruit, and the seed is a furnace.

## Fact table (all claims web-checked)

| claim | value | source |
|---|---|---|
| crust thickness | 5–70 km (VO rounds to the ~30 km textbook average) | National Geographic "Core"/Earth structure; Wikipedia "Crust (geology)" |
| mantle thickness | ~2,900 km of silicate rock, solid but flowing | Wikipedia "Mantle (geology)" |
| outer core | liquid iron-nickel, ~2,260 km thick, 4,000–5,000 °C | Wikipedia "Earth's outer core"; Sciencing (2022); EBSCO |
| magnetic field | generated by convection of the molten iron-nickel (the geodynamo) | USGS "How does the Earth's core generate a magnetic field?"; Wikipedia "Earth's magnetic field" |
| inner core | SOLID iron-nickel ball, radius ~1,220 km, surface temp ≈ 5,700 K — "about the temperature at the surface of the Sun" | Wikipedia "Earth's inner core"; National Geographic "Core" |
| why solid at that heat | pressure (~3.6 million atm) raises iron's melting point — "the entire rest of the planet and its atmosphere prevents the iron from melting" | National Geographic "Core"; Wikipedia "Earth's inner core"; Discover Magazine (2025) |

On-screen numeric labels carry the sourced ranges (4,000–5,000 °C etc.); the VO
carries rounded values only. The crust is drawn with minimum visible thickness
(all cutaway diagrams exaggerate it); every other layer is TRUE proportion
(mantle 0.911 u, outer 0.690 u, inner 0.383 u on the 2.0-unit globe — computed
from real radii).

## Scenes (the camera table lives in the TSX; sketch here)

- **S1 (0–138f, ivory light):** the settled globe (frame 0 fully composed),
  graticule turning slowly, top-left mono tag "EARTH · A CUTAWAY". Line 1. The
  wedge opens ON the act turn (t≈3.6s, easeInOutCubic 1.3s) — the portal.
  Camera: locked at [0, 0.6, 7.2] fov 34, one 1.02 settle push during line 1.
- **S2 (138–435f, teal night, continuous):** enter the wedge; camera descends
  'lin' through the mantle (label MANTLE · 2,900 KM arrives on "three thousand")
  → the outer core (label OUTER CORE · LIQUID Fe-Ni · 2,260 KM on "liquid iron";
  ivory churn particles rise) → churn peaks → field lines bloom on "magnetic
  field" (ivory arcs, scale 1.0→1.28) → camera settles before the core, HOLDS.
  Chapter-gate: the layer the voice names brightens (emissive 0.0→0.6), others
  hold 0.
- **S3 (435–780f, night, continuous):** the gold inner core fades up in the
  pause before line 5 (emissive 0→0.75 — the brightest thing in the film). Line 5
  lands; on "sun's surface" the EXPLODE opens (OFF table: crust +2.55y, mantle
  +1.65y, outer +0.78y, inner 0; settle overshoot 5%) — the camera pulls back
  and tilts to take in the vertical stack; labels arrive with their parts
  (alternating sides); 1.2s scored silence at max separation with a deep impact;
  line 6; reassembly MIRRORS the open exactly; wedge closes onto the S1 framing
  (globe whole again, graticule); line 7 + final 1.0→1.03 breathing push.

## SFX plan (cues follow the controls)

- wedge open/close: airy continuous slice, gain follows d(open)/dt
- dive: filtered riser, cutoff follows camera depth (z)
- label landings: soft ticks
- churn: low liquid rumble, amplitude follows the churn-particle phase
- field bloom: soft glassy shimmer
- explode: deep impact + reversed swell on the payoff word (in the pause)
- reassemble: the impact reversed
- close: the wedge slice, resolved
